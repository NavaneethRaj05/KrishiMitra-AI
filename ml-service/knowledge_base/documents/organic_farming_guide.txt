ORGANIC FARMING GUIDE — NCOF (NATIONAL CENTRE OF ORGANIC FARMING)

INTRODUCTION
Organic farming is a production system that maintains and improves the health of soils, ecosystems, and people through use of natural inputs and elimination of synthetic chemicals. In India, organic farming is growing rapidly — over 3.9 million ha under certified organic cultivation. Key markets: domestic health food, export (Europe, USA, Japan).

COMPOSTING
Composting converts farm waste (crop residues, animal dung, kitchen waste) into a stable, nutrient-rich soil amendment. Good compost provides all macro and micronutrients plus beneficial microorganisms.

Pit composting method: Dig a pit 2–3 m × 1.5 m × 1 m deep. Alternate 15 cm layers of green material (crop residue, leaves) and 5 cm layers of fresh dung. Sprinkle with water to maintain moisture at 50–60%. Turn the pile every 30 days for aeration. Compost is ready in 90–120 days when dark brown, crumbly, and earthy-smelling.

Application rate: 10–15 t/ha for general crops; 20–25 t/ha for vegetables. Incorporate into soil 2–4 weeks before planting.

Vermicomposting: Use Red Earthworms (Eisenia fetida). Layer 15 cm bedding material, add kitchen/crop waste, maintain 60–70% moisture. Harvest after 45–60 days when worm casts dominate the bed. Apply 2–3 t/ha. Provides higher nitrogen and phosphorus than ordinary compost.

GREEN MANURE CROPS
Green manures fix atmospheric nitrogen and add organic matter when ploughed under. Sow before the main crop or as intercrops.

Recommended species:
- Dhaincha (Sesbania bispinosa): fastest growing, fixes 100–150 kg N/ha in 45–60 days. Ideal for rice-based systems.
- Sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea): deep-rooted, drought-tolerant, fixes 80–100 kg N/ha.
- Cowpea: dual-purpose (grain + green manure), fixes 60–80 kg N/ha.
- Berseem (Egyptian clover): winter green manure, fixes 50–70 kg N/ha.
- Cluster bean (Guar): drought-tolerant, suitable for dryland areas.

Plough under at 50% flowering stage when biomass and nitrogen content are highest.

BIO-PESTICIDES
1. Neem-based products: Neem oil (2–3 mL/L + 1 mL/L liquid soap as emulsifier) controls aphids, whiteflies, mites, leaf miners, and young caterpillars. Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%) is effective against over 200 insect pests. Apply every 7–10 days.

2. Pseudomonas fluorescens: Biocontrol agent against soil-borne diseases (Fusarium wilt, Pythium damping-off). Seed treatment: 10 g/kg seed. Soil drench: 2.5 kg/ha mixed in 500 L water.

3. Trichoderma viride/harzianum: Suppresses root rot, wilt, stem rot caused by Fusarium, Pythium, Rhizoctonia. Seed treatment 4–6 g/kg; soil application 2–3 kg/ha mixed in 50 kg FYM.

4. Beauveria bassiana: Entomopathogenic fungus controlling aphids, whiteflies, stem borers. Apply 1–2 kg/ha foliar spray in evening.

5. Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): Specific to Lepidopteran caterpillars (armyworms, bollworms, helicoverpa). Apply 1–2 kg/ha when larvae are small (1st–2nd instar).

6. Panchagavya: Mixture of cow dung (1 kg), cow urine (200 mL), milk (200 mL), curd (200 mL), ghee (50 g) fermented for 7 days. Dilute 1:10 and spray as foliar stimulant/growth promoter every 15 days.

CROP ROTATION
Principles: Alternate cereals with legumes to replenish nitrogen. Deep-rooted crops follow shallow-rooted crops. Alternate heavy feeders with light feeders. Break pest and disease cycles by changing host crop each season.

Example rotations:
- Rice → Wheat → Pulses (most common in North India)
- Maize → Chickpea → Groundnut (dryland Karnataka/AP)
- Sugarcane (ratoon) → Onion → Green gram
- Cotton → Wheat → Moong → Sorghum

Benefits: Reduces chemical inputs by 30–40%, improves soil structure, breaks weed cycles, reduces pest and disease pressure.

CERTIFICATION AND MARKETING
PGS India (Participatory Guarantee System): Government scheme for small farmers. Group certification. Low cost. Recognised for domestic market.

NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production): For export markets. Third-party certification required. USDA Organic and EU Organic equivalence recognised.

Premium prices: Organic produce fetches 20–100% premium over conventional. Aggregation through FPOs (Farmer Producer Organisations) strengthens bargaining power.
