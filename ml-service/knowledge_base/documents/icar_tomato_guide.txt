ICAR Tomato Production Guide — Global Reference

INTRODUCTION
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is one of the most important vegetable crops globally.
It is grown extensively across South Asia, Southeast Asia, Africa, and Latin America in
both Kharif/wet (June-September) and Rabi/dry (October-February) seasons.

SOIL AND CLIMATE
Tomato grows well in well-drained red sandy loam or black loam soils with pH 6.0–7.0. 
The crop requires a temperature range of 20–32°C. Excessive humidity above 85% promotes 
fungal diseases. Rainfall of 600–1200 mm annually is suitable.

VARIETIES
Recommended varieties for tropical/subtropical conditions:
- Arka Vikas: High yielding, suitable for Kharif/wet season
- Arka Rakshak: Disease resistant, hybrid, recommended for small farmers
- COTH-3: Tolerant to early blight and bacterial wilt
- PKM-1: Good for fresh market, medium duration
- Pusa Ruby, Roma (suitable for processing)
- Local hybrids adapted to specific agro-climatic conditions

LAND PREPARATION
Deep plough the field 2-3 times. Apply 25 tonnes of well-rotted farmyard manure (FYM) 
per hectare before transplanting. Form raised beds or ridges for better drainage.

NURSERY AND TRANSPLANTING
Sow seeds in nursery beds at 600g per hectare. Transplant 25-30 day old seedlings at 
60cm x 45cm spacing (main season) or 45cm x 30cm (short season). Transplant in the 
evening to reduce transplanting shock.

FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
Apply NPK at 120:60:60 kg per hectare as basal dose. Top dress with 40 kg N per hectare 
at 30 days after transplanting and again at 45 days. Micronutrient spray of 0.5% zinc 
sulfate at 15 and 30 days after transplanting improves fruit quality.

IRRIGATION
Irrigate immediately after transplanting. Subsequently irrigate every 4-5 days in summer 
and 7-10 days in winter. Drip irrigation saves 40% water compared to flood irrigation 
and reduces foliar disease incidence. Critical irrigation stages: transplanting, flowering, 
fruit development, and fruit ripening.

DISEASE MANAGEMENT

Early Blight (Alternaria solani):
Symptoms: Circular brown spots with concentric rings (target-board pattern) on older leaves.
Management: 
- Spray Mancozeb 75% WP at 2g/L water at 10-day intervals
- Remove and destroy infected leaves
- Avoid overhead irrigation
- Use resistant varieties like COTH-3

Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans):
Symptoms: Water-soaked lesions on leaves turning brown-black with white fungal growth.
Management:
- Spray Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) at 2.5g/L
- Improve air circulation by proper spacing
- Avoid excessive nitrogen application

Fusarium Wilt:
Symptoms: Yellowing of lower leaves, wilting despite adequate soil moisture, brown 
discoloration of vascular tissue.
Management:
- Use wilt-resistant varieties
- Soil treatment with Carbendazim 1g/L before transplanting
- Crop rotation with non-host crops for 3 years

Tomato Leaf Curl Virus:
Transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci). Use Imidacloprid 70% WG at 5g per 15L water 
to control whitefly. Use virus-free seedlings.

PEST MANAGEMENT
Fruit borer (Helicoverpa armigera): Major pest. Use pheromone traps (5/ha) for monitoring. 
Spray Spinosad 45% SC at 2.5ml per 10L water or Emamectin benzoate 5% SG at 0.5g/L.

HARVESTING
Fruits are ready 60-80 days after transplanting depending on variety. Harvest when 
fruits turn light pink to red. Average yield: 25-30 tonnes per hectare for hybrid varieties, 
15-20 tonnes for open-pollinated varieties.

MARKET
Peak production months vary by climate zone: January-March and July-September in tropical areas.
Sell through local APMC / mandi, online platforms (eNAM in India), or cooperative markets.
Price range: Rs 500-3000/quintal in India; equivalent local market rates in other countries.
Check Agmarknet (agmarknet.gov.in) for real-time mandi prices across India.
