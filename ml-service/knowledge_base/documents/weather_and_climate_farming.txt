Weather, Climate and Farming Advisory Guide

MONSOON FARMING CALENDAR FOR KARNATAKA

Southwest Monsoon (Kharif Season)
Onset: Karnataka coast by June 1-5, interior by June 10-15.
Withdrawal: October.
Kharif sowing window: June 1 - July 15 for most crops.
Expected rainfall zones:
- Coastal/Malnad: 3000-5000mm (Dakshina Kannada, Kodagu, Shivamogga)
- Northern Karnataka: 500-750mm (Bijapur, Gulbarga)
- Hassan District: 1000-1300mm

Northeast Monsoon / Rabi Season (October-February)
Rabi sowing: October-November.
Provides 10-25% of annual rainfall in interior Karnataka.
Important for rabi crops, potato, onion, wheat in northern Karnataka.

WEATHER IMPACT ON CROP DECISIONS

High Rainfall (>150mm in 3 days):
- Stop irrigation immediately
- Apply drainage channels in field
- Risk of root rot, Pythium damping off in vegetables
- Risk of blast in rice during tillering
- Avoid pesticide spray (wash-off effect)
- Re-apply fungicides 48 hours after heavy rain

Dry Spell (No rain for 15+ days during Kharif):
- Irrigate using available water sources
- Apply anti-transpirants (Kaolin 6% spray) to reduce water stress
- Mulching with dry crop residues reduces soil moisture loss by 40%
- Skip fertilizer application until rain/irrigation available
- Focus on critical stages: flowering and grain filling need moisture most

Temperature Extremes:
Cold wave (<10°C): Risk to tomato, chilli, banana. Cover with agro-nets. Delay irrigation to morning.
Heat wave (>42°C): Risk of flower drop in cotton, tomato. Spray 1% KNO3 (potassium nitrate) or 0.5% MgSO4 to reduce heat stress. Irrigate in morning and evening.
Foggy conditions: Increase disease risk (botrytis, downy mildew). Spray copper-based fungicide preventively.

WEATHER-BASED PEST ALERTS
High humidity + Warm temperatures (>25°C): 
- High risk of fungal diseases (blast, blight, downy mildew)
- Aphid, whitefly population explosion
- Monitor fields daily; spray preventive fungicide

After prolonged dry spell followed by rain:
- White grub attack on roots (especially groundnut, sugarcane)
- Armyworm attack on maize and sorghum
- Scout fields within 3 days of rain

CROP WEATHER REQUIREMENTS

Paddy (Rice):
Germination: 20-30°C. Vegetative: 25-35°C. Flowering: 24-30°C (pollen inundation above 35°C or below 17°C).
Water requirement: 1000-2000mm. Sensitive to waterlogging at seedling stage.

Ragi:
Optimum temperature: 20-30°C. Drought tolerant but sensitive to frost.
Rainfall needed: 700-1000mm. Can grow on 500mm with moisture conservation.

Maize:
Germination: 18-25°C. Flowering: 21-27°C. Sensitive to frost and waterlogging.
Rainfall: 600-900mm well distributed.

Cotton:
Optimal: 21-30°C. Sensitive to frost and waterlogging.
Rainfall: 600-1200mm. Needs dry weather at harvest (October-November).

Tomato:
Optimal: 21-27°C day, 15-20°C night. Above 35°C causes flower drop.
Sensitive to frost. Rain during fruit set increases cracking.

Potato:
Optimum: 15-20°C. Above 30°C inhibits tuber formation. Frost kills foliage.
Rainfall: 400-600mm during growing season.

HOW TO GET WEATHER FORECASTS
1. IMD website: mausam.imd.gov.in — 5-day forecast by district
2. Meghdoot app (IMD) — free, designed for farmers, 5-day forecast with farming advisory
3. Damini app — lightning alert (important for field workers during thunder)
4. Kisan Mausam SMS service — SMS "MAUSAM <district>" to 7738299899
5. DD Chandana and DD Kisan weather bulletins: 6:30 AM and 7:30 PM

RAINFALL MEASUREMENT
Standard rain gauge: 20mm diameter, read at 8:30 AM daily.
Apps: Rainlog, IMD rainfall app.
Simple field gauge: use a measuring cylinder and straight-sided container.
1mm rainfall = 1 liter of water per square meter = 10,000 liters per hectare.

DROUGHT MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES
1. Select drought-tolerant varieties: Ragi GPU-45, Groundnut TAG-24, Maize DHMH-107
2. Deep ploughing before Kharif (25-30 cm) improves water infiltration
3. Broad bed furrow (BBF) planting for water harvesting on rainfed land
4. Farm pond construction: 30m x 30m x 3m pond stores 2.7 million liters
5. Rainwater harvesting: tie ridge method between fields
6. Micro-irrigation: Drip saves 40-60% water, sprinkler saves 30-40%
7. Mulching: Black plastic mulch reduces evaporation 60-70%
8. Contingency crop planning: If monsoon delayed by >4 weeks, switch to short-duration crops (black gram, cowpea 60-70 days) or fodder crops
