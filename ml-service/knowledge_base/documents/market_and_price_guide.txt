Agricultural Market Prices and Marketing Guide for Karnataka Farmers

UNDERSTANDING APMC (AGRICULTURAL PRODUCE MARKET COMMITTEE)
APMC markets (mandis) are regulated markets where farmers bring their produce for auction. Buyers (traders, processors) bid for produce. A licensed commission agent (arhatia) facilitates the sale and charges 1-2% commission. Karnataka has 157 APMC markets and 353 sub-markets.

PRICE FORECASTING AND MARKET INTELLIGENCE
Price cycles for major crops:
Tomato: Peaks in May-June (summer) and September-October (post-monsoon), drops in December-February (peak supply from Kolar and Hassan).
Onion: Prices low during Kharif harvest (Oct-Dec), high in May-July (lean season). Karnataka onion mostly exported to Maharashtra and North India.
Potato: Low prices in February-March during harvest season, higher in July-September.
Arecanut: Prices relatively stable due to high demand and slow production growth. Festival seasons push prices up.

HOW TO GET BETTER PRICES
1. Grading and sorting: Grade produce into A, B, C quality. A-grade fetches 20-40% premium.
2. Clean packaging: Use clean gunny bags or crates. Damaged packaging lowers perceived quality.
3. Sell at peak hours: Most mandis have peak activity 8am-12pm when more buyers are present.
4. Join FPO (Farmer Producer Organization): Collective selling gives bargaining power. Karnataka has 300+ FPOs.
5. Storage and timing: Store grains (ragi, maize) for 2-3 months post-harvest to get 20-30% better price. Use WDRA-registered warehouses and get warehouse receipts for pledge loans.
6. Direct marketing: Sell to supermarkets (More, Reliance Fresh) or HOPCOMS (Horticulture Producers Cooperative Marketing and Processing Society) directly.
7. Value addition: Dried tomatoes, ragi flour, coffee powder, coconut oil — fetch 2-5x higher value.
8. e-NAM: Register and participate in electronic auctions to reach pan-India buyers.
9. Export: Karnataka mango, coffee, and fresh ginger are exported. Contact APEDA (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority).

KISAN RAILWAY AND FREIGHT SUBSIDY
50% freight subsidy on horticulture produce transport by rail. Minimum consignment: 10 quintal. Book through nearest railway goods shed. Reduces transportation cost for distant markets.

COLD STORAGE AND WAREHOUSING
Potato: Store at 2-4°C, 90-95% humidity. Cold chain available at KSCMF and private warehouses.
Onion: Store in well-ventilated, dry godowns. Do not store wet onions.
Tomato: Short shelf life (7-10 days at ambient). Evaporative cool chambers (zero energy cool chambers) extend shelf life to 3 weeks.
Cost of cold storage: Rs. 200-400/quintal per month.

AGRI-ENTREPRENEUR OPPORTUNITIES IN KARNATAKA
1. Custom Hiring Centre (CHC) for farm machinery — subsidy of 40-80% under SMAM scheme
2. Rural Godown construction — NHB and NABARD subsidy of 25-33.33%
3. Primary processing unit (grading, cleaning) — subsidy under PMKVY and NHM
4. Organic certification — PKVY scheme covers cost of PGS/third-party certification
