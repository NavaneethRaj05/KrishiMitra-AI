INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT AND CROP PROTECTION GUIDE — INDIA

SOURCE: National Centre for Integrated Pest Management (NCIPM), New Delhi; ICAR-NIPHM, Hyderabad
AUTHORITY: Ministry of Agriculture, Government of India; ICAR

=============================================================
IPM PRINCIPLES — INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
=============================================================

DEFINITION: IPM is a sustainable approach combining biological, cultural, physical, and chemical tools to minimize economic, health, and environmental risks.

DECISION THRESHOLD — ECONOMIC THRESHOLD LEVEL (ETL):
- Action is taken only when pest population reaches ETL
- Spray at ETL — not prophylactically (prevents resistance)

=============================================================
APHID MANAGEMENT (Aphis gossypii, Myzus persicae, etc.)
=============================================================

CROPS AFFECTED: Cotton, Vegetables, Potato, Mustard, Wheat

SYMPTOMS:
- Clusters of small soft-bodied insects on young shoots, underside of leaves
- Leaves curl, yellow, distort
- Sticky honeydew secretion → sooty mold (black coating)
- Ant attendance on colonies

ECONOMIC THRESHOLD: 5-10 aphids/leaf tip OR 15-25% plants infested

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Imidacloprid 17.8% SL: 0.5 ml/litre (systemic, very effective)
- Thiamethoxam 25% WG: 0.5 g/litre
- Acetamiprid 20% SP: 0.2 g/litre
- Dimethoate 30% EC: 2.0 ml/litre (contact + systemic)
- Monocrotophos 36% SL: 1.5 ml/litre (restricted — only when ETL crossed)

ORGANIC CONTROL:
- Neem Oil 5000 ppm: 5 ml/litre + 1 ml liquid soap
- Azadirachtin 0.03%: 5 ml/litre
- Garlic extract: 50g garlic crushed in 1L water, dilute 10x, spray

BIOLOGICAL CONTROL:
- Chrysoperla carnea (green lacewing): 50,000 eggs/acre (2 releases)
- Coccinella septempunctata (ladybird beetle): conserve natural populations
- Syrphid fly larvae: natural predators

=============================================================
WHITEFLY MANAGEMENT (Bemisia tabaci)
=============================================================

CROPS AFFECTED: Cotton, Tomato, Vegetables, Chilli, Bhendi

SIGNIFICANCE: Major virus vector — transmits Leaf Curl Virus in tomato and cotton.

CHEMICAL CONTROL (rotate to prevent resistance):
- Imidacloprid 17.8% SL: 0.5 ml/litre
- Diafenthiuron 50% WP: 1.0 g/litre
- Buprofezin 25% SC: 1.5 ml/litre (IGR — disrupts nymph development)
- Spiromesifen 22.9% SC: 1.0 ml/litre (effective against nymphs and eggs)
- Pyriproxyfen 10% EC: 1.0 ml/litre (IGR)

CULTURAL/PHYSICAL:
- Yellow sticky traps: 10-15 per acre (monitoring + mass trapping)
- Silver/reflective mulch: 25 micron silver-colored plastic mulch (repels whitefly)
- 50-mesh nylon net in nursery

=============================================================
THRIPS MANAGEMENT (Thrips tabaci, Scirtothrips dorsalis)
=============================================================

CROPS AFFECTED: Onion, Chilli, Cotton, Grapes, Potato

SYMPTOMS:
- Silver/bronze streaking of leaves
- Leaf tips rolled with feeding scars
- Distorted flowers and fruits
- Onion: leaves turn silvery then die

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Spinosad 45% SC: 0.3 ml/litre (most effective biological insecticide)
- Fipronil 5% SC: 2.0 ml/litre
- Lambda-cyhalothrin 5% EC: 1.0 ml/litre
- Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG: 0.4 g/litre

CULTURAL CONTROL:
- Blue sticky traps for thrips: 10-15 per acre
- Drench with Fipronil 5% SC 3 ml/litre for soil-dwelling pupae

=============================================================
MITE MANAGEMENT (Tetranychus urticae, Tetranychus cinnabarinus)
=============================================================

CROPS AFFECTED: Chilli, Brinjal, Okra, Grapes, Apple, Rose

SYMPTOMS:
- Bronze/rust colored leaves; webbing on underside
- Tiny red/yellow/green mites visible with magnifying glass
- Leaf distortion and drop in severe cases

CHEMICAL CONTROL (acaricides):
- Abamectin 1.9% EC: 0.5 ml/litre (highly effective)
- Dicofol 18.5% EC: 2.5 ml/litre
- Spiromesifen 22.9% SC: 1.0 ml/litre
- Propargite 57% EC: 2.0 ml/litre
- Fenazaquin 10% EC: 2.0 ml/litre (miticide)
NOTE: Mites develop resistance quickly — rotate acaricides.

BIOLOGICAL:
- Predatory mites: Neoseiulus californicus, Phytoseiulus persimilis

=============================================================
STEM BORER MANAGEMENT — GENERAL
=============================================================

CROPS AFFECTED: Rice (Scirpophaga), Maize, Sugarcane, Jowar

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Cartap hydrochloride 50% SP: 1.5 g/litre OR 8 kg/acre granule in rice
- Chlorpyrifos 20% EC: 2.5 ml/litre (spray into whorl)
- Fipronil 0.3% GR: 8 kg/acre (into standing water in rice)
- Monocrotophos 36% SL: 1.5 ml/litre (into whorl of maize)

BIOLOGICAL CONTROL:
- Trichogramma japonicum: 1 lakh eggs/acre (egg parasitoid)
- Cotesia flavipes: Larval parasitoid for sugarcane borer
- Tetrastichus howardi: Larval parasitoid for stem borers

=============================================================
FUNGICIDE RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT
=============================================================

ANTI-RESISTANCE STRATEGIES:
1. ROTATE fungicide classes: Never use same mode of action twice in a season
2. MIXTURES: Use pre-mixed multi-site fungicides (e.g., metalaxyl + mancozeb)
3. ALTERNATION SCHEDULE:
   - Spray 1: Mancozeb (M3 group — multi-site)
   - Spray 2: Metalaxyl+Mancozeb (systemic + contact)
   - Spray 3: Cymoxanil + Mancozeb
   - Spray 4: Copper oxychloride (M1 — multi-site)

FUNGICIDE GROUPS (by mode of action):
- MBC group (Carbendazim, Thiophanate-methyl): Risk of resistance — alternate
- Triazoles (Propiconazole, Tebuconazole, Hexaconazole): Medium risk — rotate
- Strobilurins (Azoxystrobin, Trifloxystrobin): High risk — never use alone
- SDHI (Fluxapyroxad, Boscalid): High risk — always in mixture
- Multi-site (Mancozeb, Chlorothalonil, Copper): LOW risk — backbone of programs

=============================================================
GOVERNMENT SUBSIDY ON PESTICIDES — INDIA
=============================================================

SUBSIDY PROGRAMS:
- NMOOP (National Mission on Oilseeds): 50% subsidy on certified pesticides for oilseed crops
- Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture (MIDH): Subsidy on plant protection equipment
- PM-Krishi Sinchayee Yojana: Subsidy on drip/sprinkler + fertigation

HOW TO APPLY:
- Visit nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) with land documents
- Apply through state agriculture department portal
- Kisan Call Centre: 1800-180-1551 (toll-free, 24x7)

KEY SAFETY RULES:
- Wear PPE: gloves, mask, boots during spraying
- Do not spray during hot afternoon hours (evaporation loss, heat stroke risk)
- Best spray timing: early morning (6-9 AM) or late evening (4-6 PM)
- Pre-harvest interval (PHI): always observe — do not spray close to harvest
- Dispose empty containers safely — do not reuse for food/water storage
