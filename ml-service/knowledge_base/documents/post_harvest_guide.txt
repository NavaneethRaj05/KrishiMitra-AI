POST HARVEST MANAGEMENT GUIDE — ICAR-CIPHET LUDHIANA

INTRODUCTION
India loses 15–18% of food grains and 25–40% of fruits and vegetables annually due to poor post-harvest handling. Proper storage, grading, and packaging can recover Rs 90,000 crore worth of food per year. Post-harvest management begins at the field — correct timing of harvest is as important as storage conditions.

HARVESTING AT CORRECT MATURITY
Cereals: Harvest wheat and rice at 18–22% grain moisture. Avoid harvesting at high moisture (>25%) as it promotes mould. Delayed harvest causes shattering losses. Combine harvesters reduce field losses to 1–2% vs 3–5% for manual harvest.

Vegetables: Harvest in the morning after dew has dried. Most vegetables should be harvested at physiological maturity, not over-ripe. Tomatoes for local market: pink-red stage. For distant markets: green-turning stage. Leafy vegetables: before bolting. Onions: 50% tops down.

Fruits: Mangoes: harvest when specific gravity reaches 1.01–1.02 (float test). Bananas: harvest at 75% filling. Citrus: full colour development. Grapes: 20–24° Brix (sugar content).

FIELD COOLING AND HANDLING
Temperature management after harvest is critical. Every 10°C reduction in temperature roughly doubles shelf life. Shade harvested produce immediately. Avoid harvesting during peak heat (10 AM–4 PM) for perishables.

Hydrocooling (cold water): Effective for leafy vegetables and cucumbers. Precooling reduces field heat rapidly.

Forced air cooling: Standard for grapes, strawberries, stone fruits. Achieves 90% cooling in 2–4 hours.

GRADING AND SORTING
Remove diseased, damaged, and undersized produce. Grading improves market value by 15–30%. Hand-grading: sufficient for small quantities. Mechanical graders: available for tomato, potato, onion, mango through horticultural co-operatives.

Grade standards for major commodities (APEDA/AGMARK):
- Mango: Extra, Grade A (>300g), Grade B (200–300g), Grade C (<200g)
- Tomato: Grade I (no defects, 57–75 mm), Grade II (minor defects)
- Onion: 35–50 mm, 50–75 mm, >75 mm grades

PACKAGING
Primary packaging: Direct contact with produce. Use food-grade materials. Ventilated corrugated boxes for most fruits and vegetables. Perforated polyethylene bags for moisture-sensitive crops. Avoid re-using old packaging (disease carry-over risk).

Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP): Reduces oxygen, increases CO2 inside package. Extends shelf life of leafy vegetables by 2–3× at ambient temperature. Available cost-effectively for high-value crops.

CFB (Corrugated Fibre Board) boxes: Standard for export. APEDA specifications required for export produce.

STORAGE SYSTEMS
Covered storage: Clean, dry warehouse. Stacking on pallets/racks (10 cm off floor). Maintain temperature 10–15°C for most grains. Relative humidity 65–70%. Treat with malathion 0.1% before storage.

Cold storage: Essential for potato (2–4°C, 90–95% RH), apple (0–2°C), grapes (−1°C to 0°C), tomato (10–13°C). Community cold storage available through State Horticulture Departments at rental.

Hermetic storage: Airtight bags (PICS bags, ZeroFly) create low-oxygen atmosphere, effectively controlling storage pests without chemicals. Suitable for grains (wheat, maize, pulses). One-season use typically.

Onion storage: Spread onions in bamboo/wooden structures with good ventilation. Top layer: dry onions. Avoid moisture. Maintain 65–70% RH, 25–35°C. Inspect weekly and remove rotten bulbs.

MINIMISING LOSSES IN TRANSPORT
Use refrigerated trucks for long-distance transport of perishables. Pre-cool the vehicle before loading. Avoid over-loading. Use vibration-absorbing materials (paddy straw, wood shavings) between layers. Drive during cool hours. Do not mix commodities with different ethylene sensitivities (e.g., apples with cucumbers).

VALUE ADDITION
Processing reduces waste and improves income:
- Tomato: ketchup, paste, puree (Rs 25–40/kg raw → Rs 80–120 finished product)
- Mango: pickle, pulp, juice, aam papad
- Onion: dehydrated flakes (high export value)
- Turmeric: polished fingers, powder
FPO-level mini processing units available under PMFME scheme with 35% government subsidy.
