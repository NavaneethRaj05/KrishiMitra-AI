RICE CULTIVATION GUIDE — ICAR-NRRI CUTTACK

INTRODUCTION
Rice (Oryza sativa) is the staple food crop of India. It is grown in three seasons: Kharif (June–November), Rabi (November–April), and Zaid (March–June). India produces approximately 120 million tonnes annually. Transplanted paddy is the dominant cultivation method in most rice-growing states.

LAND PREPARATION
Thoroughly puddle the field 2–3 times after flooding to achieve a mud consistency that suppresses weed germination. Final levelling with a laser leveller saves 25–30% irrigation water. For direct-seeded rice, prepare a firm, well-drained seedbed. Incorporate green manure (Sesbania) or crop residue at last tillage.

SEED SELECTION AND NURSERY MANAGEMENT
Choose certified seeds of recommended varieties. Soak seeds in water for 24 hours, then incubate wrapped in moist gunny for 24–48 hours until germination. Treat seeds with Carbendazim 2g/kg or Pseudomonas fluorescens 10g/kg to prevent seedling diseases.

Nursery rate: 20–25 kg seed/ha for transplanted rice; 80–100 kg/ha for direct seeding. Raise nursery in well-prepared raised beds (10 cm height, 1.2 m width). Apply Cartap hydrochloride 4G @ 20 kg/ha in nursery at 10 days after sowing (DAS) to control leaf folder and stem borer. Nursery should be 21–25 days old before transplanting.

TRANSPLANTING
Transplant 2–3 seedlings per hill at 15 × 15 cm spacing for short-duration varieties, 20 × 15 cm for medium-duration varieties. Avoid deep planting — shallow planting at 2–3 cm depth promotes early tillering. Transplant in the afternoon to reduce transplanting stress.

WATER MANAGEMENT
Maintain 2–5 cm standing water from transplanting through tillering. Drain field 5–7 days before panicle initiation to suppress unproductive tillers (mid-season drainage). Maintain 2–3 cm water during panicle initiation to flowering (most critical stage). Drain 15 days before harvest to allow field to dry.

Alternate wetting and drying (AWD): Allow soil to dry until 15 cm below field surface before re-irrigating. Saves 25–30% water with no yield loss. Use a simple field water tube to monitor water table depth.

FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
Apply fertilizers based on soil test. General recommendation for high-yielding varieties:
- Nitrogen (N): 120 kg/ha split in 3 doses: 40% basal + 30% at active tillering (21 DAT) + 30% at panicle initiation.
- Phosphorus (P2O5): 40 kg/ha as basal.
- Potassium (K2O): 40 kg/ha, half basal and half at panicle initiation.
- Zinc sulphate: 25 kg/ha as basal where zinc deficiency is known.

Urea super granules (USG) placed 5 cm deep at transplanting improve nitrogen use efficiency by 30–40% compared to broadcast urea.

WEED MANAGEMENT
Critical weed competition period is 0–45 days after transplanting. Apply pre-emergence herbicide (Butachlor 50% EC at 2.5 kg a.i./ha) at 3–5 DAT in 2–3 cm standing water. Follow with one manual weeding at 30–35 DAT. Cono-weeder between rows at 20 and 40 DAT reduces weed pressure in direct-seeded rice.

VARIETY SELECTION
Short-duration (110–125 days): MTU 1010, IR 64, Pooja
Medium-duration (130–145 days): Swarna (MTU 7029), Mahsuri, BPT 5204
Long-duration (>145 days): Jaya, Ratna (for specific ecosystems)
For rainfed upland: Vandana, Sahbhagi Dhan (drought-tolerant)
For saline soils: Luna Barial, CST 7-1

HARVESTING
Harvest when 80–85% grains reach golden yellow colour (moisture content 20–22%). Use combine harvester or power reaper. Thresh within 24 hours of cutting to minimise field losses.

YIELD TARGETS
Transplanted irrigated paddy: 5–7 t/ha (improved varieties with good management)
Direct-seeded rainfed paddy: 3–4 t/ha
