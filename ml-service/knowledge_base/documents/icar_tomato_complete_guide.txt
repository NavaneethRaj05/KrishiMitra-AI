ICAR COMPREHENSIVE TOMATO DISEASE AND PEST MANAGEMENT GUIDE

SOURCE: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR), National Institute for Plant Health Management (NIPHM)
AUTHORITY: ICAR-IARI, Pusa Institute, New Delhi

=============================================================
TOMATO EARLY BLIGHT (Alternaria solani)
=============================================================

SYMPTOMS:
- Dark brown concentric ring spots (target-board pattern) on older, lower leaves
- Lesions have yellow chlorotic halo around them
- Lesions enlarge and coalesce, causing complete leaf death
- Stems may show dark elongated lesions near soil line
- Fruit develops dark sunken lesions at stem end

CAUSE: Fungal pathogen Alternaria solani. Spreads through infected plant debris, rain splash, and wind.
FAVORABLE CONDITIONS: Temperature 24-29°C, Relative Humidity above 80%, frequent rains.

CHEMICAL CONTROL (per litre water):
- Mancozeb 75% WP: 2.5 g/litre (spray every 7-10 days)
- Chlorothalonil 75% WP: 2.0 g/litre (preventive spray)
- Iprodione 50% WP: 1.5 g/litre (for severe infection)
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC: 1.0 ml/litre (systemic fungicide)
- Difenoconazole 25% EC: 0.5 ml/litre (curative fungicide)

ORGANIC/BIO CONTROL:
- Trichoderma viride: 4 g/kg seed treatment
- Bacillus subtilis: 5 g/litre foliar spray
- Neem Oil (5000 ppm): 5 ml/litre + 1 ml liquid soap, every 5-7 days
- Copper oxychloride 50% WP: 3 g/litre (copper-based preventive)

MANAGEMENT PRACTICES:
- Remove and destroy all infected plant debris after harvest
- Crop rotation: avoid tomato in same field for 2-3 years
- Use certified disease-free seeds; treat seeds with Thiram 75% WS @ 3g/kg
- Stake plants properly for air circulation
- Avoid overhead irrigation — use drip irrigation
- Plant resistant varieties: Arka Rakshak (IIHR), Pusa Hybrid 1

=============================================================
TOMATO LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora infestans)
=============================================================

SYMPTOMS:
- Water-soaked, irregular pale green spots rapidly turning dark brown/black on leaves
- White sporulation (cottony growth) on underside of leaves in humid conditions
- Lesions appear first on leaf tips and margins
- Stems show dark brown lesions causing stem collapse
- Fruits show firm dark brown lesions, rapidly decaying

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS: Temperature 15-21°C, RH above 85%, cool wet weather. Spreads extremely fast.

CHEMICAL CONTROL (per litre water):
- Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP: 2.5 g/litre (best for late blight — systemic)
- Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP: 3.0 g/litre
- Dimethomorph 50% WP: 1.5 g/litre
- Propamocarb 72.2% SL: 1.5 ml/litre

SPRAY INTERVAL: Every 5-7 days during epidemic conditions. Alternate products to prevent resistance.
WARNING: Do NOT spray during or just before rain. Wait for dry conditions.

ORGANIC CONTROL:
- Copper oxychloride 50% WP: 3 g/litre (effective preventive)
- Potassium bicarbonate 0.5%: foliar spray
- Neem leaf extract: 50g leaf boiled in 1L water, cooled, strained, spray

=============================================================
TOMATO LEAF CURL VIRUS (ToLCV)
=============================================================

SYMPTOMS:
- Upward or downward curling and rolling of leaves
- Yellowing and thickening of leaf veins
- Stunted, bushy plant growth
- Mosaic pattern on leaves
- Severely infected plants produce no fruit

CAUSE: Begomovirus transmitted by Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci). Cannot be treated — ONLY PREVENTED.

VECTOR CONTROL (Whitefly management):
- Yellow Sticky Traps: 10-15 per acre (monitor and mass trap)
- Imidacloprid 17.8% SL: 0.5 ml/litre — spray on whitefly colonies
- Thiamethoxam 25% WG: 0.5 g/litre
- Dimethoate 30% EC: 2.0 ml/litre
- Azadirachtin 0.03% (Neem EC): 5 ml/litre — organic option

INTEGRATED MANAGEMENT:
- Uproot and destroy severely infected plants immediately to stop spread
- Plant marigold as border/trap crop to repel whitefly
- Use silver/reflective mulch to confuse whitefly
- Install 50-mesh nylon net in nursery to exclude whitefly
- Resistant varieties: Arka Rakshak, Arka Samrat (IIHR Bangalore)

=============================================================
TOMATO FRUIT BORER (Helicoverpa armigera)
=============================================================

SYMPTOMS:
- Round entry holes on fruits; fruits drop prematurely
- Caterpillar feeds inside fruit — may enter at calyx end
- Wilting of growing tips

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG: 0.4 g/litre
- Spinosad 45% SC: 0.3 ml/litre (bio-derived, low toxicity)
- Chlorpyrifos 20% EC: 2.0 ml/litre (older chemical option)
- Lambda-cyhalothrin 5% EC: 1.0 ml/litre

BIOLOGICAL CONTROL:
- Helicoverpa NPV (HaNPV): 250 Larval Equivalents (LE) per acre
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): 1.0-1.5 kg/acre
- Pheromone traps: 5-8 per acre (Helilure pheromone) for monitoring
- Egg parasitoid Trichogramma chilonis: 1 lakh eggs/acre, 2-3 releases

=============================================================
FERTILIZER SCHEDULE FOR TOMATO
=============================================================

TOTAL NPK REQUIREMENT (per acre):
- Nitrogen (N): 60 kg
- Phosphorus (P): 60 kg
- Potassium (K): 60 kg

APPLICATION SCHEDULE:
- Basal (at planting): FYM 10 tonnes/acre + DAP 130 kg + MOP 30 kg + Urea 20 kg
- 15-20 days after transplanting: Urea 33 kg (first top dress)
- 30-40 days after transplanting: Urea 33 kg + MOP 70 kg (second top dress)
- Micronutrients: Borax 10 kg/acre (basal) for flower drop prevention

FOLIAR NUTRITION (for high yield):
- 00:52:34 (Mono Potassium Phosphate): 5 g/litre at flowering
- 0:0:50 (SOP): 5 g/litre at fruit development
- Calcium nitrate: 2 g/litre during fruit setting to prevent Blossom End Rot
- 13:00:45 (Potassium Nitrate): 5 g/litre at fruit maturity for quality

=============================================================
CROP CALENDAR FOR TOMATO — INDIA
=============================================================

KHARIF (Rainy season): June-July planting; Harvest September-November
RABI (Winter season): October-November planting; Harvest January-March (Best quality season)
SUMMER season: January-February planting; Harvest April-June

RECOMMENDED VARIETIES BY REGION:
- Karnataka: Arka Rakshak, Arka Samrat, NS-524
- Maharashtra: Phule Raja, Vaibhav, Abhinav
- Andhra Pradesh/Telangana: US-2853, Pusa Ruby
- Punjab/Haryana: Pusa Hybrid 2, Punjab Kesari
- Tamil Nadu: PKM-1, CO-3, Arka Saurabh
