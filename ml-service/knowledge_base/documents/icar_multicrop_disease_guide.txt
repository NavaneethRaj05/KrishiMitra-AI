ICAR CROP DISEASE MANAGEMENT — MAIZE, WHEAT, POTATO, COTTON, GROUNDNUT

SOURCE: ICAR-Indian Institute of Maize Research (IIMR), ICAR-IARI, NIPHM
AUTHORITY: National Centre for Integrated Pest Management (NCIPM), New Delhi

=============================================================
MAIZE DISEASES
=============================================================

MAIZE GRAY LEAF SPOT (Cercospora zeina / C. zeae-maydis)
SYMPTOMS:
- Long, narrow rectangular lesions confined between leaf veins
- Lesions: initially yellow-green, becoming tan/grey with yellow halo
- Lesions run parallel to veins, giving "dried grass blade" appearance
- Severe infection: entire leaf dies prematurely

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS: Warm (25-30°C), high humidity, cloudy weather, continuous maize cropping.

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Propiconazole 25% EC: 1.0 ml/litre (most effective triazole fungicide)
- Tebuconazole 25.9% EC: 1.0 ml/litre
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC + Propiconazole 11.7%: 1.0 ml/litre (combination best)
- Chlorothalonil 75% WP: 2.0 g/litre (protectant)
SPRAY TIMING: At tasseling (VT) and silking (R1) stages

BIOLOGICAL CONTROL:
- Trichoderma asperellum: seed treatment 4 g/kg
- Pseudomonas fluorescens: foliar spray 5 g/litre

MAIZE COMMON RUST (Puccinia sorghi)
SYMPTOMS:
- Circular to elongated brick-red pustules on both leaf surfaces
- Pustules break open, releasing brick-red powdery spores
- Severe infection causes early leaf death

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Propiconazole 25% EC: 1.0 ml/litre (triazoles very effective against rust)
- Tebuconazole 25.9% EC: 1.0 ml/litre
- Hexaconazole 5% EC: 2.0 ml/litre
- Mancozeb 75% WP: 2.5 g/litre (protectant)
SPRAY AT: First appearance of pustules

MAIZE NORTHERN LEAF BLIGHT (Exserohilum turcicum)
SYMPTOMS:
- Long cigar-shaped grey-green lesions (5-15 cm long)
- Lesions turn tan/brown as they age; may have dark borders
- Start on lower leaves, move upward
- Distinguishable from gray leaf spot by elongated boat-shaped lesions

CHEMICAL CONTROL: Same as Gray Leaf Spot — propiconazole, tebuconazole, azoxystrobin.

MAIZE FALL ARMYWORM (Spodoptera frugiperda) — HIGH PRIORITY PEST
SYMPTOMS:
- Window-pane feeding on young leaves
- "Pinholes" in leaves progressing to ragged tears
- Frass (sawdust-like) inside whorl
- Larva has inverted Y on head, 4 black spots on 8th abdominal segment

CHEMICAL CONTROL (emergency):
- Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG: 0.4 g/litre (most effective)
- Spinetoram 11.7% SC: 0.5 ml/litre
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC: 0.4 ml/litre (Coragen — premium option)
- Thiodicarb 75% WP: 1.0 g/litre (stomach poison)

BIOLOGICAL CONTROL:
- Spodoptera frugiperda NPV (SfNPV): 250 LE/acre (spray at evening)
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): 1.5 kg/acre
- Trichogramma pretiosum: 1 lakh eggs/acre

MAIZE FERTILIZER SCHEDULE (per acre):
- Total NPK: N-60 kg, P-30 kg, K-20 kg
- Basal: DAP 65 kg + MOP 33 kg + Urea 20 kg
- 1st Top Dress (25-30 DAS): Urea 43 kg
- 2nd Top Dress (45-50 DAS): Urea 43 kg

=============================================================
WHEAT DISEASES
=============================================================

WHEAT YELLOW RUST (Puccinia striiformis)
SYMPTOMS:
- Bright yellow powdery pustules arranged in stripes along leaf veins
- Yellow stripes run parallel — distinguishable from brown rust's scattered pustules
- Causes yield loss of 10-70%

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS: Cool temperature 10-15°C, high humidity, heavy dew.

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Propiconazole 25% EC: 1.0 ml/litre (seed to boot stage)
- Tebuconazole 25.9% EC: 0.5 ml/litre
- Hexaconazole 5% EC: 2.0 ml/litre
SPRAY AT: First appearance; repeat after 15 days if needed.

WHEAT BROWN RUST (Puccinia triticina)
SYMPTOMS:
- Round-oval orange-brown pustules scattered randomly on upper leaf surface
- Pustules contain orange-brown uredospores
- Most common rust of wheat

CHEMICAL CONTROL: Same as yellow rust — propiconazole or tebuconazole.

WHEAT POWDERY MILDEW (Blumeria graminis)
SYMPTOMS:
- White powdery patches on upper leaf surface
- Powdery growth later turns grey-brown
- Occurs in cool, humid, shaded conditions

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Carbendazim 50% WP: 1.0 g/litre
- Propiconazole 25% EC: 1.0 ml/litre
- Sulfex (Wettable Sulphur 80% WP): 3.0 g/litre

WHEAT FERTILIZER SCHEDULE (per acre):
- Total: N-50 kg, P-25 kg, K-12 kg
- Basal: DAP 55 kg + MOP 20 kg + Urea 10 kg (at sowing)
- 1st Top Dress (21 DAS, crown root initiation): Urea 55 kg
- 2nd Top Dress (42 DAS, jointing stage): Urea 45 kg (if needed)

=============================================================
POTATO DISEASES
=============================================================

POTATO LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora infestans)
SYMPTOMS:
- Dark water-soaked lesions on leaves, rapidly turning brown-black
- White downy sporulation on leaf underside in humid conditions
- Severe infection: entire field destroyed within 5-7 days
- Tubers: brown-rot with irregular reddish-brown internal discoloration

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS: Temperature 10-25°C, RH above 90%, cool and wet.
Most destructive potato disease — caused Irish Famine 1845.

CHEMICAL CONTROL (most important — act FAST):
- Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP: 2.5 g/litre (systemic — BEST option)
- Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP: 3.0 g/litre
- Fluopicolide 39.35% SC: 0.3 ml/litre
- Copper oxychloride 50% WP: 3.0 g/litre (protectant, use preventively)
SPRAY INTERVAL: Every 5-7 days during high-risk weather.

POTATO EARLY BLIGHT (Alternaria solani)
SYMPTOMS:
- Dark brown concentric ring target-board spots (same as tomato early blight)
- Appears on older lower leaves first
- Yellow halo around lesion

CHEMICAL CONTROL: Mancozeb 2.5 g/litre OR Chlorothalonil 2.0 g/litre, every 10 days.

=============================================================
COTTON DISEASES AND PESTS
=============================================================

COTTON BOLLWORM (Helicoverpa armigera)
- Most devastating cotton pest
- Larva feeds on flower buds (squares), flowers, and bolls
- Entry holes on bolls with frass

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG: 0.4 g/litre
- Spinosad 45% SC: 0.3 ml/litre
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC: 0.4 ml/litre
BIOLOGICAL: HaNPV 250 LE/acre, Trichogramma spp. 1 lakh/acre

COTTON PINK BOLLWORM (Pectinophora gossypiella)
- Pink larvae inside cotton bolls
- Causes boll rot and reduced lint quality
- Pheromone traps (Gossyplure): 5 per acre for monitoring

COTTON WILT (Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. vasinfectum)
- Sudden wilting; internal vascular discoloration (reddish-brown)
- Soil-borne — no cure once infected
- Prevention: Seed treatment with Trichoderma 4 g/kg + Carbendazim 2 g/kg
- Resistant varieties: NHH-44, Suraj, Bunny BG-II

GROUNDNUT DISEASES
=============================================================

TIKKA/CERCOSPORA LEAF SPOT (Cercospora arachidicola & C. personatum)
SYMPTOMS:
- Circular dark brown spots on leaves (Tikka = spots)
- Early leaf spot: brown spots with yellow halo on upper surface
- Late leaf spot: dark brown spots on lower surface, no halo

CHEMICAL CONTROL:
- Chlorothalonil 75% WP: 2.0 g/litre
- Mancozeb 75% WP: 2.5 g/litre
- Tebuconazole 25.9% EC: 1.0 ml/litre
SPRAY at 30, 45, 60 and 75 DAS

GROUNDNUT STEM ROT / WHITE MOLD (Sclerotium rolfsii)
- Water-soaked lesion at stem base, white fluffy growth
- Small, round, hard brown sclerotia on soil surface
- Apply Trichoderma viride 2.5 kg/acre + FYM 50 kg mixed 15 days before sowing

GROUNDNUT FERTILIZER SCHEDULE (per acre):
- Total: N-8 kg, P-16 kg, K-16 kg + Gypsum 100 kg at pegging (most important)
- Basal: SSP 100 kg + MOP 27 kg
- Gypsum: 100 kg/acre at pegging stage (critical for pod filling)
